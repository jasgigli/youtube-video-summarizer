## 🔬 In-Depth Analysis

## The Limits of Copper
- Copper lines served early telecommunications well, but struggled with capacity constraints over distance.
- High-frequency signals face the 'skin effect', which restricts current flow to the outer edges of the conductor and increases resistance.

## The Promise and Peril of Glass
- Light-based communication through glass offered a massive bandwidth upgrade over copper.
- Initial attempts failed because standard commercial glass was overwhelmingly 'lossy'—absorbing and scattering light before it could travel meaningful distances.

## Overcoming Impurities
- Signal attenuation in glass is primarily caused by impurities (like transition metal ions) and microscopic imperfections.
- Breakthroughs in materials science enabled the creation of ultra-pure silica glass, reducing impurities to parts-per-billion levels.

## Manufacturing the Modern Internet
- Producing fiber optic glass requires highly specialized chemical vapor deposition and precise drawing towers to stretch the glass into hair-thin strands without introducing defects.
- These innovations created the physical backbone that underpins global telecommunications and the modern internet.
